Dietro le Quinte

Backstage Behind the Curtain

Enrico Januzzi never existed and the 1975 production never happened. These are the people who actually made it.

Regia · Musica · Montaggio

Eric Jackowitz

alias Enrico Januzzi, and half of Fantasma Nero (with Franky Fox)

Eric is a Miami-born musician turned filmmaker who started out behind a drum kit. He went through Berklee College of Music, played with George Clinton, Blake Mills and Nick Waterhouse, and co-founded the bands The Cooties and Wolves of Glendale.

He moved into filmmaking in 2018 with music videos for his band Cape Weather. That road led to his debut short, The Seeing Eye Dog Who Saw Too Much. Influenced by Dario Argento, John Carpenter, Quentin Tarantino and Spike Jonze, he mixes music, humour and genre. Like Carpenter, he edits his own films and writes their music; he co-scored this one with Franky Fox. He wants his films to stay with audiences long after the lights come up.

Produzione

Colton Mastro

alias Constanzo Mastroduzzio

Colton grew up in Arizona, where his grandmother took him to the movies every Saturday night with a smuggled Pepsi and a bag of popcorn. Afterwards she made him explain what he did or didn't like about the film, and those conversations started his love of story and character.

He earned degrees in Business Management and Marketing, then completed the Professional Program at UCLA's School of Theater, Film and Television. He has produced twelve features and shorts, which have played more than 40 festivals and won awards along the way. He also writes and directs, and he gravitates to character-driven stories with a distinct look.

L'Etichetta

Sono Bloccato

Distributori Associati S.p.A.

Sono Bloccato is the creative partnership of Eric Jackowitz and Colton Mastro, built on a shared taste for bold genre stories. It began in 2021 with Delivery, a short Colton wrote and starred in and asked Eric to co-direct. Delivery premiered at the Cleveland International Film Festival, won the Audience Award at the Phoenix Film Festival, and took Best Directors at WorldFest Houston.

A few years later Eric brought Colton on to produce The Seeing Eye Dog Who Saw Too Much, and the partnership became a label for strange, singular genre projects.

Il Restauro

WBAZ Films

the restoration house that does not exist

WBAZ Films "found" the footage, "restored" it and "presents it for the very first time". The restoration was all deliberate: the film was shot on film, and no sound was recorded on set. Every line was dubbed in post, the way 1970s Italian productions did it.
